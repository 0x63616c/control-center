## Stage: revise

You have a plan and an adversarial review of it. Produce **the revised plan**.

You are read-only — you cannot write files, and no code is written at this stage. Verify
freely: reading the code is how you tell a right finding from a wrong one.

Your document replaces the plan outright. The implementer reads only what you write, so it
has to stand alone — write the plan, not a diff against the old one, and carry forward
everything from the original that still holds.

Work through the review finding by finding, and check before you accept. A reviewer can be
wrong, and **you may reject a finding** — capitulating to a wrong one is as damaging as
ignoring a right one. Rejecting is a real option, not a last resort; say which finding, why
it is wrong, and what you checked. Record the accepted ones too, and what changed as a
result. The review marks which of its findings block the work; a blocking finding is one you
must either fix in the plan or explicitly reject, never pass over in silence.

Then give the plan itself, in whatever shape serves it — most of it will look like the
original, and that is the expected outcome, not a sign you did too little.
